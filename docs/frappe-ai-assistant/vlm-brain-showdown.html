The VLM Brain Showdown

Qwen2.5-VL is the baseline. Who actually beats it on the box we can afford?

Target ~16 GB self-hosted Contenders 5 open-weight VLMs Rule free until proven

Our assistant needs one model that can read structured data, write SQL, extract fields from scanned invoices, and describe a photo of a rose. Qwen2.5-VL-7B is our proven baseline — it serves cleanly on a quantized ~16 GB box and already answers text and image prompts. The question Spec A exists to settle is simple: is there a better brain that still fits? Below are the real contenders as of mid-2026, what the public benchmarks actually say, and the constraint that quietly decides everything — VRAM.

The contenders

Where MiMo pulls ahead: reading charts & documents

For an ERP assistant, the benchmark that matters most is the hard one about reading charts and dense documents — exactly the skill needed to pull totals off a scanned invoice. On CharXiv-RQ (reasoning questions over scientific charts, higher is better), Xiaomi's 7B model opens a real gap over both Qwen and InternVL — despite being the same size as Qwen.

✓Why this is the headline chart: reading a scanned invoice is chart/document reasoning. A 14-point lead here is the single most decision-relevant gap in the whole comparison — and it comes from a model the same size as our baseline.

The baseline is no pushover

Before crowning a challenger, respect the incumbent. Qwen2.5-VL-7B's own document and OCR profile is genuinely strong — this is the bar every rival has to clear on our eval set, not just on a leaderboard.

The constraint that decides it: VRAM

Benchmarks pick the smartest model. Your budget picks the one you can actually run. A 7B model in full precision is roughly 15 GB of weights alone — which leaves no room for the KV cache on a 16 GB card. So on the cheap box, a quantized build isn't optional:

▲Fits today, quantized: Qwen2.5-VL (AWQ), GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking (AWQ), InternVL3-9B. Won't fit in full precision: MiMo-VL-7B-RL and Pixtral-12B — they need a community AWQ/GPTQ quant, or the ~24 GB reference tier. "Best on benchmarks" and "fits the budget" are not always the same model — and that tension is itself a finding worth putting in front of the decision.

Every candidate speaks the same OpenAI-compatible API, so swapping is a one-line change — serve, run the eval set, stop, serve the next:

vllm serve dengcao/GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking-AWQ \
  --max-model-len 8192 --gpu-memory-utilization 0.92 --port 8000 \
  --limit-mm-per-prompt '{"image":4}'

The verdict — for now

The real answer comes from our own ~40-question eval set on synthetic ERP data, not a public leaderboard. But the shape of the race is clear: Qwen2.5-VL is the safe, well-fitting baseline; GLM-4.1V is the easiest rival to actually test at this VRAM tier; and MiMo-VL is the one to beat on document understanding — if we can squeeze it onto the box. Next step: build the scorecard and let execution accuracy, OCR field-match, and measured latency settle it.

→What this page is not: a final scorecard. These are public benchmarks that guide which models are worth loading. The verdict comes from Stage 2 self-hosted runs on our own data.
